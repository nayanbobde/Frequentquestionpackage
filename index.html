FABRIC IN PASSAGE

Microsoft Fabric is Microsoft's unified end-to-end analytics platform that brings together Data Factory, Data Engineering, Data Warehouse, Data Science, Real-Time Analytics, and Power BI into a single SaaS solution. Before Fabric, organizations typically used multiple separate services such as Azure Data Factory for ETL, Azure Synapse for warehousing and analytics, Azure Data Lake for storage, and Power BI for reporting. Managing multiple tools increased complexity, integration effort, security management, and cost.

Fabric solves this by providing a single platform with OneLake as the centralized storage layer. OneLake acts like OneDrive for enterprise data, allowing all Fabric workloads to access the same data without creating multiple copies.

One of the key concepts in Fabric is the Lakehouse, which combines the flexibility of a Data Lake with the performance of a Data Warehouse. It can store structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data while supporting both SQL and Spark workloads.

For reporting, Fabric introduces Direct Lake mode, where Power BI reads data directly from OneLake. This provides near Import-mode performance without needing to duplicate data or rely on slower DirectQuery queries.

Fabric also supports Dataflow Gen2 and Data Factory pipelines for ETL processes, Warehouses for SQL analytics, and Real-Time Analytics for streaming and log data. Since everything is integrated into one platform, organizations get better governance, simplified architecture, reduced maintenance, improved performance, and faster development cycles.

Although my primary hands-on experience is with Power BI, I understand how Fabric unifies the modern data platform and enables scalable analytics through OneLake, Lakehouse, Warehouses, and Direct Lake integration.

1. SQL — Allocation & Inventory Analysis

One of the major challenges I faced was that some stores were experiencing stockouts even though inventory was available across the network. My responsibility was to identify whether the root cause was demand, supply or allocation. I used SQL joins, aggregations and business logic across inventory, allocation, store and DC data and compared the inventory allocated to each DC against the stores they were serving. For example, I identified that one DC was receiving around 70% of the inventory while serving around 50% of the stores, indicating an allocation imbalance. My analysis supported the 60/30/10 rebalancing recommendation, which was associated with approximately 30% reduction in stockouts and 10% improvement in allocation accuracy.

2. Power BI — KPI, DAX & Data Modeling

One of the Power BI challenges was maintaining accurate and consistent KPI calculations across different business views. I was responsible for building a reliable model and ensuring KPIs such as WOC, Stockout Rate, In-Stock %, Sell-Through and Allocation Accuracy were calculated correctly. I followed a Star Schema, created the required relationships and developed reusable DAX measures, and then validated the Power BI results against SQL outputs. For example, I used WOC to identify stores with low inventory coverage and potential stockout risk versus stores with excess inventory, helping planners make better allocation and replenishment decisions. This resulted in a more reliable and consistent reporting layer for the business.

3. SQL + Power BI — Performance Optimization

Another challenge was maintaining good dashboard performance while working with retail sales, inventory and allocation data. I optimized the SQL Views, data model and DAX calculations, reduced unnecessary data and primarily used Import Mode for the core dashboards because they had nightly refresh requirements. For example, instead of performing every KPI calculation inside Power BI, I standardized reusable reporting logic in SQL Views such as vw_kpi_master and then consumed the prepared data in Power BI. For the specific requirement of real-time DC stock monitoring, I used DirectQuery. This helped create a performance-focused reporting architecture while still meeting the business requirements.

4. Inter-Store Transfer — Inventory Rebalancing

Another challenge was that some stores had excess inventory while other stores had shortages of the same product. I was responsible for identifying potential Inter-Store Transfer opportunities before recommending additional procurement. I used SQL to compare product-level inventory across stores and applied conditions such as SOH greater than 100 for excess stores and below 20 for needy stores, while keeping a safety-stock buffer at the source store. For example, if Store A had excess units of a product while Store B had very low stock, the analysis could identify Store A → Store B as a potential transfer. This created a structured approach to inventory rebalancing and stockout prevention.

5. KPI/Data Validation — SQL vs Power BI

One challenge was ensuring that the numbers shown in Power BI were consistent with the underlying SQL data. When a KPI or total did not match, I traced the data flow from the SQL base tables and Views through Power Query, the Power BI model and DAX measures. For example, for an inventory KPI, I would compare the SQL-level aggregation with the Power BI result and check for duplicate records, incorrect relationships, filters or transformation issues. I then corrected the relevant logic and revalidated the result. This helped ensure that the dashboards provided accurate and trusted numbers to business users.

Project Story

One of the projects I'm most proud of was the Global Direct SAR (Spend Analysis Report) Dashboard developed for the finance leadership team. The goal was to provide a single source of truth for company-wide spending across regions, departments, and cost categories.

There were two major challenges. First, the company followed a custom fiscal year starting in August instead of January, so all YTD, Budget YTD, and Prior Year calculations had to align with that fiscal calendar. Second, the data came from multiple regions and currencies, requiring consistent currency conversion across the organization.

To solve this, I implemented the currency conversion logic in SQL views. For completed months, the month-end exchange rate was used, and for the current month, the previous month's closing rate was applied. This ensured stable financial reporting.

In Power BI, I kept Power Query transformations minimal for better refresh performance and developed DAX measures for Fiscal YTD Actuals, Budget YTD, and Prior Year comparisons based on the August fiscal calendar.

I also implemented dynamic Row-Level Security using USERPRINCIPALNAME() and a role mapping table. Executives could see all data, SET-level users could view their functional area, and department users could only access their own data and relevant report pages.

As a result, the finance team moved away from manual monthly reconciliation and started using a trusted dashboard where each user automatically saw the data relevant to their role.

Biggest Challenge – Revenue Dashboard Issue

One of the most challenging situations I faced was with an existing revenue dashboard. At a summary level, the revenue numbers appeared correct, but when users drilled down to the product group level, the figures didn't match the source system.

This was a critical issue because the finance team was using those numbers for margin analysis and business decisions.

I performed a detailed analysis of both the data model and DAX measures. During the investigation, I found that some table relationships were not designed correctly, and a few measures were not handling filter context properly.

I redesigned the data model following star schema principles, corrected the relationships, and rewrote the affected DAX measures to ensure accurate calculations at every drill-down level.

After implementing the changes, I worked closely with the finance team and validated the results against source data row by row until every number matched.

After the fix, the business gained confidence in the dashboard and stopped performing manual Excel-based validation for margin analysis, which significantly improved reporting efficiency.
